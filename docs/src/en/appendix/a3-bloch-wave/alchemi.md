This page defines the quantities computed by [7.4. ALCHEMI simulation](../../7-diffraction-simulator/4-alchemi-simulation.md). The wave field in the crystal is obtained with the same Bloch-wave method as the [dynamical core](calculation.md).

## Wave field

For each incident orientation, the amplitude of reflection $\mathbf g$ is expanded as

$$
\psi_{\mathbf g}(z) = \sum_j \alpha_j\, C_{\mathbf g}^{(j)}\, e^{2\pi i \gamma_j z},
$$

where $\gamma_j$ are the Bloch-wave eigenvalues, $C_{\mathbf g}^{(j)}$ the eigenvectors and $\alpha_j$ the excitation amplitudes fixed by the boundary condition at the surface. Below, $D = C\,\mathrm{diag}(\alpha)$, that is $D_{\mathbf g j} = C_{\mathbf g}^{(j)}\alpha_j$.

## Ionization matrix

For each pair of site $s$ and ionization channel $c$, an ionization matrix $\mu_{s,c}$ is built. Its elements depend only on the difference of two reflections, $\mathbf G = \mathbf g_h - \mathbf g_g$:

$$
\mu_{hg} = \sum_{a \in s} \mathrm{Occ}_a\, e^{-M_a(\mathbf G)}\, \sigma_c\, F_c(|\mathbf G|/2)\, e^{-2\pi i\,\mathbf G \cdot \mathbf r_a}
$$

- $\sigma_c$ : total ionization cross section (**Bote–Salvat** model)
- $F_c(s)$ : normalized ionization form factor (DHFS tables generated by **Temari**; the same tables as [STEM-EDX](../../9-hrtem-stem-simulator/2-stem-simulation.md))
- $e^{-M_a(\mathbf G)}$ : Debye–Waller factor (anisotropic displacements are supported)

For an X-ray line series, $\sigma_c$ is $\sum_i B_i\sigma_i$ over its subshells $i$ and $F_c$ is the $B_i\sigma_i$-weighted mean of their form factors, where $B_i$ is the fluorescence yield × line branching after the Coster–Kronig and radiative cascades (xraylib 4.2.1). This is the **local form-factor approximation** of ICSC (Oxley & Allen 2003); the two-momentum MDFF is not used.

## Channelled yield

The ionization yield up to thickness $t$ (per incident electron) is

$$
Y_\text{dyn}(t) = \frac{1}{V_c}\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{jj'} \bigl(D^{\dagger}\mu_{s,c}D\bigr)_{j'j}\,F_{jj'}(t)
= \frac{1}{V_c}\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{jj'} \alpha_{j'}^{*}\bigl(C^{\dagger}\mu_{s,c}C\bigr)_{j'j}\,\alpha_j\,F_{jj'}(t),
$$

$$
F_{jj'}(t) = \int_0^t e^{\lambda z}\,dz = \frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda},
\qquad \lambda = 2\pi i\,\bigl(\gamma_j - \overline{\gamma_{j'}}\bigr).
$$

$V_c$ is the unit-cell volume, which converts $\mu$ (an area) per unit cell into a yield per incident electron. In the degenerate limit $\lambda \to 0$, $F_{jj'}(t) \to t$. The row (bra) index is $j'$ and the column index is $j$.

## Dechannelled component

Electrons removed from the coherent Bloch field by thermal-diffuse absorption travel the remaining thickness as randomly directed electrons and ionize there as well:

$$
Y_\text{dech}(t) = \frac{\mu_{00}}{V_c}\,\bigl(t - L_\text{coh}(t)\bigr),
\qquad
L_\text{coh}(t) = \int_0^t \sum_{\mathbf g} \frac{P_{\mathbf g}}{P_0}\,\bigl\lvert\psi_{\mathbf g}(z)\bigr\rvert^2\,dz.
$$

$\mu_{00} = \sum_a \mathrm{Occ}_a\,\sigma_c$ is the diagonal of $\mu$. $P_{\mathbf g}$ is the component of the wave vector of wave $\mathbf g$ along the surface normal ($P_0$ for the incident wave), so $\sum_{\mathbf g}(P_{\mathbf g}/P_0)\lvert\psi_{\mathbf g}\rvert^2$ is the coherent current flowing along the surface normal. Without absorption this quantity is exactly 1, $L_\text{coh}(t) = t$ and the dechannelled component vanishes ($\sum_{\mathbf g}\lvert\psi_{\mathbf g}\rvert^2$ equals 1 only when $P_{\mathbf g} \approx P_0$). $L_\text{coh}$ is computed with the same contraction, using $\mu = \mathrm{diag}(P_{\mathbf g}/P_0)$.

The total yield is $Y = Y_\text{dyn} + Y_\text{dech}$. Because $t - L_\text{coh}(t)$ collects all current lost to absorption, only thermal-diffuse absorption may be re-injected (when other absorption is mixed in, ReciPro stops the calculation and reports it).
